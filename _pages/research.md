# Research
## Understanding and treating refractory seizure disorders. 
Seizure disorders includes chronic epilepsy as well as severe presentations such as status epilepticus
Refractory defined as unable to be controlled after two therapeutic trials of ASMs
There are many theories related to the mechanisms of refractoriness, none of which in isolation completely account for the phenomenon, but many of which in combination likely do contribute. 
    
We favor the two-part theory that 1) ASM failure may be due to the limited target diversity of existing ASMs and that 2) refractory seizure disorders possess unique pathophysiology. 
    
Most ASMs discovered by blind compound screening in phenotypic screens; hits identified by an increase in the seizurogenic threshold (e.g. phenytoin, cat model) where a healthy animal is induced to seize through chemical (proconvulant) or electrical stimulation. 

Highly productive at identifying compounds which incidentally appear to have efficacy in most human individuals with chronic epilepsy (~60%) or status epilepticus (also ~60%).  But due to their discovery from acute seizure models, the efficacy of these compounds may be predicated on many unobserved parameters having normal physiological values. 

In patients with refractory conditions there are evidently deviations from these expected normal conditions or their dynamics, likely due to a combination of: 1) genetics (large vs small risk alleles), and/or 2) acquired pathology at the cellular/molecular level. 

Unlike in other areas of medicine (e.g. oncology), where extensive efforts have been made to identify new targets to control disease, there has been comparatively little attention to this question in seizure disorders -- perhaps due to the preconception that our understanding of nervous system function and seizure disorders is sufficiently complete as to obviate the need for new hypothesis-generating experiments.  
    
These considerations lead us to the main overarching interests of the lab to: 
1. develop novel models of refractory seizure disorders for mechanistic insights and for screening 
2. characterize the molecular determinants that modulate seizurogenesis and the efficacy of existing ASMs
3. Neurotherapeutic development.
